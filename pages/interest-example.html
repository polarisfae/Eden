Kintsugi, and Liking Broken Things Mended Visibly

planted 4 June 2025 · branching still
craftrepairobsessions

I got interested in kintsugi the way most people do — a photo of a bowl with gold seams, and a caption about beauty in breakage. But what's kept me reading, years later, is smaller and less quotable than that: the seam doesn't hide the break, it dates it.

You can look at a well-mended piece and read its history — which drop happened first, which crack ran fastest, which repair was done by a steadier hand. Nothing about the object pretends it was never broken. It just keeps being useful, visibly changed.

Why it keeps branching

This one interest has quietly pulled in a handful of others: Japanese lacquer work, visible mending in textiles (sashiko is doing a very similar thing with thread instead of gold), and — a little unexpectedly — the argument for keeping first drafts around instead of deleting them. Same idea, different material.
