On Leaving Things Half-Finished

planted 12 March 2024 · still blooming
writinggardensunfinished things

There is a particular kind of guilt reserved for the unfinished draft. It sits in a folder, half-argued, waiting for a conclusion that never quite arrives, and every so often I open it and feel obscurely at fault — as if a sentence, once begun, owes the world its ending.

I don't think that's true anymore. Or rather, I think it's only true of certain kinds of writing — the kind built to be handed over, finished, checked off. A garden asks something different of you. Nobody expects a rosebush to apologize for still being a bud in April.

A thought doesn't have to be resolved to be worth keeping. It just has to be alive.

What "still blooming" actually means

When I plant something here, I try to date it honestly and then leave the ending open. Some of these pages will get quietly rewritten a year from now, once I've thought about them longer. Others will stay exactly as they are — a first take, a little rough at the edges, and none the worse for it.

The point of a digital garden, as I understand it, isn't to publish conclusions. It's to let the in-between state be visible: the seedling next to the bloom, the branch that never quite became anything, the note that's really just a scrap. Half-finished isn't a failure state here. It's just early.
